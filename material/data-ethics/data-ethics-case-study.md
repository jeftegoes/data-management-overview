# 1. Data Ethics Case Study Workbook

## 1.1. Apply DAMA-DMBOK2 Principles to Real Scenarios

- This workbook is designed to help learners apply the ethical principles defined in the DAMA-DMBOK2 to real-world data challenges.

# 2. Ethical Principles Reference

- **Fairness** - Avoid bias or discrimination in data processes.
- **Transparency** - Be open about how and why data is used.
- **Accountability** - Assign responsibility for data decisions.
- **Consent** - Obtain informed permission for data use.
- **Privacy** - Protect individuals' rights and information.
- **Stewardship** - Manage data as a valuable, trusted asset.
- **Lawfulness** - Follow all applicable laws and regulations.

# 3. Case Study 1 - Customer Consent and Purpose Limitation

- **Scenario**
  - A retail company wants to reuse customer purchase data to predict new product interests.
  - The original consent form did not mention marketing analytics.

## 3.1. Discussion Questions

- What ethical principle is at risk?
- What actions could the company take to remain compliant?
- What governance control should flag this issue?

## 3.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Principle:** Transparency and Consent.
- **Action:** Obtain explicit new consent or anonymize data before reuse.
- **Control:** Data Governance Council or Privacy Officer review.

# 4. Case Study 2 - Algorithmic Bias in Hiring

- **Scenario**
  - An HR analytics team uses a historical recruitment dataset to train an AI screening model.
  - It rejects more women than men.

## 4.1. Discussion Questions

- What ethical issues are present?
- Which DMBOK principle applies?
- How could governance mitigate bias risk?

## 4.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Issues:** Bias, lack of fairness and inclusivity.
- **Principle:** Fairness and Accountability.
- **Action:** Review training data, apply bias detection, document results, and report findings.

# 5. Case Study 3 - Social Listening and Privacy

- **Scenario**
  - Your marketing team mines social media posts to identify customer sentiment, including personal comments and usernames.

## 5.1. Discussion Questions

- Which ethical boundaries are being crossed?
- What principle(s) are in conflict?
- What is an acceptable ethical alternative?

## 5.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Issue:** Misuse of public personal data without consent.
- **Principle:** Privacy and Respect for Individuals.
- **Action:** Aggregate or anonymize data; ensure compliance with platform policies.

# 6. Case Study 4 - Cross-Border Data Transfers

- **Scenario**
  - A financial firm transfers customer data from the EU to the US for analytics without reviewing data transfer agreements.

## 6.1. Discussion Questions

- What regulation or principle is relevant here?
- What governance process should exist?
- How can accountability be ensured?

## 6.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Principle:** Lawfulness and Accountability.
- **Control:** Data Governance Committee oversight and data transfer assessments.
- **Action:** Apply Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) and inform data subjects.

# 7. Case Study 5 - Over-Collection in Data Projects

- **Scenario**
  - A data team collects more attributes than necessary "just in case" they're useful later.

## 7.1. Discussion Questions

- Which principle is violated?
- What should governance enforce?
- What's the ethical response?

## 7.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Principle:** Stewardship and Purpose Limitation.
- **Control:** Data Collection Policy and periodic audits.
- **Action:** Limit data to the minimum required; delete unnecessary fields.

# 8. Case Study 6 - AI Explainability and Transparency

- **Scenario**
  - A bank deploys an AI model to determine loan approvals.
  - Customers are denied loans but cannot see why.

## 8.1. Discussion Questions

- What ethical issues arise?
- What principle applies?
- What corrective action should be taken?

## 8.2. Model Answers / Guidance

- **Principle:** Transparency and Accountability.
- **Action:** Provide model explanations, human review, and clear communication to customers.
